Public learning resource

PSIntelligence 101

An educational pipeline for rocket-apogee prediction, from probability and filtering to models, uncertainty and onboard deployment. The notebooks are a starting point for study; they do not establish flight qualification.

Open the notebooks
1

Start with probability and measurements

Use the introductory notebooks to connect uncertainty, noisy measurements and Kalman filtering. A sensor reading becomes more useful when you can explain what it does and does not tell you.

2

Estimate a changing flight state

Explore Kalman filters, the extended Kalman filter and the unscented Kalman filter. Compare how models and measurements contribute to an estimate.

3

Study rocket-apogee prediction

PSIntelligence introduces physics-informed models and sequence models for apogee prediction, alongside uncertainty estimation. These are learning resources and research directions.

4

Think about onboard deployment

Consider the practical step from a notebook to onboard computation: timing, limited resources and the meaning of a reliable estimate. The curriculum does not claim that every model has flown.

Learning has a place
in the week.

General meetings and research meetings serve different purposes. For the current calendar and meeting location, follow the group’s announcements.

Member seminars & preparation

Members introduce interests, projects and papers. General meetings also support preparation for launch activity, making space to connect a technical topic with the work ahead.

Research progress & discussion

Research teams share progress and prepare for conference presentations. Listening to how a team explains its assumptions, methods and results is part of learning how research works.

Coursework that
meets hardware.

PSI’s conference archive contains work on linking rocket-engineering coursework with an aerospace club and on using CFD flow visualisation for education. These are concrete examples of teaching and student projects informing one another.
