Rocket programme

PSLV

POSTECH Science Launch Vehicle

The PSLV programme connects the entire sounding-rocket workflow: vehicle construction, avionics integration, launch and recovery. Its public record includes the first-generation November 2024 flight, an August 2025 avionics flight and PSLV-II in December 2025.

Avionics

Bring the data back.

Explore the Avionics repository

A rocket carries more than a processor: it needs a coherent path from sensors to decisions and telemetry. PSI’s public flight-software project runs on the Arduino Portenta H7 and separates tasks using an RTOS.

Sensors and state estimation

The code interfaces with GNSS, an IMU and a BMP3XX barometric sensor. The current filtering implementation uses vertical acceleration and barometric altitude to estimate motion. GNSS provides positioning; it should not be mistaken for an input to that same fused state estimate.

Telemetry and ground-station software

XBee transmits measurements to a Python ground station. Its real interfaces show a 3D attitude model and flight state, a GNSS-derived path, and three-axis acceleration plots. Timestamped logs support a later look at altitude, velocity, pressure and predicted apogee.

These are capabilities visible in the public software. They are not a claim of certification, reliability or deployment of every feature on a particular flight.

Ground testing / public source

Thrust Measurement
System

From a force
to a useful record.

Explore the TMS repository

The Thrust Measurement System (TMS) connects instrumented ground testing to calibration, logs and analysis. Its value is the traceable route from a sensor signal to a measurement that can be inspected.

Acquisition and storage

An Arduino Nano reads load-cell and pressure signals through two ADS1115 channels and stores measurements on an SD card. The acquisition code configures 320 samples per second; that is distinct from the ADC’s conversion-rate setting.

Calibration and interpretation

Python tools support calibration, filtering and thrust/impulse analysis. The public test archive keeps plots and experiment notes beside processed data, including instrument anomalies and other limitations.

Aircraft programme

More than
a rocket team.

The Aircraft Team designs and fabricates quadrotor and VTOL aircraft for competition activity.

The research archive also asks how vortex generators affect flight stability and how mesostructures relate to the behaviour of small aircraft. These historical studies and current programme descriptions are different parts of the same broader interest in flight.

A research topic on reinforcement-learning-based landing was selected in April 2026. The topic is a research objective, not a claim of a demonstrated autonomous landing.
